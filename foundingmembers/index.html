A letter to our founding families · November 2026

You told us what you dream of. Here’s where it’s taking us.

The house is being imagined again from the ground up, with your answers on the drawing table.

Concept film

Hello, founding families.

Thank you. So many of you wrote back, and your answers were generous, specific and wonderfully ambitious. A family kitchen where we cook together. A pool in an orangery. Big tree houses. Outdoor rooms. Quiet corners where a parent can work while their child plays nearby.

We read every one. Together they pushed us past a list of rooms and towards a question: what kind of childhood, and what kind of parenthood, should this house make room for?

The answer is becoming our design philosophy. It will shape how the spaces are laid out, how families meet each other in them, and how the club grows over the years. This page is where we will share it, and keep you updated as it takes form.

Ida, and the team at The Bury

Getting here, getting home

Easy arrivals, easy goodbyes.

We are exploring valet parking and a pickup service for the days you need it, plus a comfortable drive home after a long evening, so arriving and leaving never becomes the hard part.

Our philosophy

A house where children and parents both learn by trying.

We don’t believe in one right way to raise a child. We believe children grow brave when they can see the people they love being brave too: trying new things, getting them wrong, and trying again. So this house is designed for children and for parents, as learners side by side.

Curiosity

Asks the question. Real books, real tools and real materials, within reach and allowed to be touched.

Creativity

Makes something in answer. Half-finished work is shown proudly on the walls, by children and parents alike.

Courage

Shows the attempt to someone else. Every busy room has a quiet corner, because courage needs somewhere safe to return to.

The heart of the house · Work in progress

How do we create a connective tissue for the whole house?

This is the question we keep coming back to with our architect. Our current thinking: the main atrium becomes a tall library, a place for curiosity, creativity and co-working all at once.

The hard part, and the fun part, is letting very different needs live side by side. These are the ideas we are exploring:

  • Quiet pockets. Small booths where a parent can take a call without leaving the room.
  • Silent reading rooms. Calm corners where children can curl up with a book.
  • Room for noise. Livelier, playful spaces where sound is welcome.
  • Sightlines, not walls. So a child can always find a parent’s face.

None of this is fixed. We are still finding the connections, and we would love your thoughts.

Co-working

Work properly. Stay close.

Some days you need a real desk, good light and a quiet hour. Other days you simply want to be near your children.

We want the co-working space to give you both. It opens off the library, so you can get on with your work while your child reads, builds or plays a few steps away, and look up whenever you like.

We are still working out the details, from how quiet each corner should be to how close is close enough. Tell us what a good working day here would look like for you.
